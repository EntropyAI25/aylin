# IP, marketing communication, accessibility and other compliance risks for a small Indian fashion/accessories e-commerce site (as of Oct 2026)

Method note: searches returned secondary sources (law-firm notes, trade press, vendor guides). I could not open statutory texts, so figures marked "verify" should be checked on indiacode.nic.in, trai.gov.in, ccpa.gov.in or the Meta policy pages before being stated as fact.

## 1. Copyright, trademark, brand images, replicas, domains

### Takeaway
Criminal exposure under both the Copyright Act and the Trade Marks Act is real (up to 3 years' jail, fine up to Rs 2 lakh, offences described as cognizable and non-bailable). In practice the common fashion-sector enforcement is civil: fast ex-parte injunctions, takedown of social handles, and domain freezes. Selling replicas or "first copy" items, or copying another brand's photos, is the highest-risk behaviour.

### Cited Findings
- Copyright Act s.63 and Trade Marks Act s.103 carry up to 3 years' imprisonment and fine up to Rs 2 lakh; sources differ on a minimum (one note and a Lok Sabha document say minimum 6 months and Rs 50,000 fine for ss.103-105; others quote only maximums) — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/litigation/anti-counterfeiting-laws-india/intellectual-property-laws-counterfeiting-india/); [Nishith Desai on Bombay HC](https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/dispute-resolution-hotline/bombay-high-court-offences-under-the-trade-marks-act-and-copyright-act-are-non-bailable-4696); [Lok Sabha doc](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/453204/1/66022.pdf)
- Offences under s.63 Copyright Act and s.103 Trade Marks Act are cognizable and non-bailable per the Bombay HC note; police may seize infringing copies under s.64 — [Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/dispute-resolution-hotline/bombay-high-court-offences-under-the-trade-marks-act-and-copyright-act-are-non-bailable-4696); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/litigation/anti-counterfeiting-laws-india/intellectual-property-laws-counterfeiting-india/)
- Copyright s.17(b): where a photograph is taken for valuable consideration at someone's instance, that person is first owner absent contrary agreement. So paying a photographer generally makes you the owner, but an unpaid friend or a free-lance shoot without paperwork is murkier; get a written assignment/licence — [Copyright Act text (India Code)](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1367/1/a195714.pdf); [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/copyright-registration-of-photographs/)
- 2025 Delhi HC fashion examples: House of Masaba obtained interim injunction against fake Instagram accounts, with direction to Instagram to take down pages and disclose defendants' details; Rahul Mishra got a "John Doe" dynamic injunction against counterfeit dresses, with registrar ordered to freeze domain and disclose registrant; Reliance Retail (Yousta) got ex-parte injunction against rogue websites/social accounts — [WTR Masaba](https://www.worldtrademarkreview.com/article/delhi-high-court-grants-injunctive-relief-house-of-masaba-in-dispute-over-fraudulent-instagram-accounts); [SCC Online Rahul Mishra](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/01/04/delhi-high-court-grants-injunction-indian-fashion-designer-rahul-mishra-counterfeit-dresses-legal-news/); [LiveLaw Yousta](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/delhi-high-court/reliance-retail-ltd-yousta-fashion-brand-trademark-infringement-rogue-websites-social-media-accounts-fraud-284544)
- Trademark filing: Rs 4,500 per class (individuals, startups, small enterprises) vs Rs 9,000 (others), e-filing; Class 25 = clothing/footwear/headgear, Class 35 = advertising/business management (retail services are generally filed here); realistic 6-18+ months to certificate (commercial sources) — [Taxaj](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=295); [Echai Ventures](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-trademark-ip/trademark-cost-and-timeline-india)

### Inferences
- Minimum safeguards: use only photos you shot or hold written licence/assignment for; keep model releases (written consent for using a person's face/likeness commercially); licence fonts, stock images and music (commercial licence, not "free for personal use"); never use supplier/brand photos without written permission; do not sell look-alikes or "replicas" carrying another brand's name or logo; file own brand in Class 25 (and 35 if you want to protect the retail service) early; check the Trade Marks Registry search and domain/handle availability before choosing a name.
- Passing off (unregistered rights in a similar name/get-up) can be sued on even without registration; so a name close to an established fashion brand is risky even if the domain is available.
- Instagram/Facebook reel music: platform library licences usually cover personal/creator accounts, not business accounts; use the business-approved audio library or licensed tracks (general knowledge, not confirmed by a source found here).

### Gaps
- Not verified: statutory text of ss.63/103 minimums; Section 57 moral rights; fair dealing limits; Indian case law specifically on music in reels or font licence enforcement; domain dispute process (INDRP via .in registry, UDRP for .com) and its costs — no source retrieved.
- Replica/"first copy" specific Indian cases not retrieved.
- Trademark opposition timeline and fee not found in sources.

## 2. WhatsApp / SMS / email marketing, DLT, DPDP consent

### Takeaway
Commercial SMS requires DLT registration of the sender and templates, with DND honoured; WhatsApp requires clear opt-in naming the business; DPDP consent obligations are due to bite from mid-May 2027 but should be built in now. Consent must be separate for marketing and easy to withdraw.

### Cited Findings
- TRAI's TCCCPR 2018 was notified 19 July 2018; it requires registration of senders/headers and templates; amended 12 Feb 2025 (spam complaint window up to 7 days, faster action vs unregistered telemarketers, curbs on 10-digit numbers for telemarketing) — [Message Central guide (vendor)](https://www.messagecentral.com/sms-guideline/india); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/trai-tightens-rules-against-spam-introduces-provision-of-stricter-penalties-56616.htm); [TRAI Direction 04052024](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/Direction_04052024.pdf)
- Promotional SMS is blocked to DND-registered numbers; unregistered traffic is blocked and may be blacklisted; a vendor mentions per-complaint fines up to Rs 1,000 (unverified) — [Exotel](https://developer.exotel.com/docs/faqs/trai-regulations)
- Financial disincentives Rs 2 lakh / 5 lakh / 10 lakh fall on telecom operators for misreporting, not on small senders — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/trai-tightens-rules-against-spam-introduces-provision-of-stricter-penalties-56616.htm)
- WhatsApp: opt-in needed for marketing/utility messages outside the 24-hour window; opt-in must state the person agrees to receive WhatsApp messages and name the business; business is responsible for legal compliance; prohibited categories include weapons, tobacco, drugs, adult products, etc. (third-party summaries, not Meta's own pages) — [Optimove](https://academy.optimove.com/hc/en-us/articles/25863306876829-Regulatory-Requirements-for-WhatsApp-Campaigns); [360dialog](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/messaging/sending-and-receiving-messages/before-sending-a-message); [Rasayel](https://learn.rasayel.io/en/books/whatsapp/whatsapp-business/whatsapp-business-messaging-policy)
- DPDP Rules 2025 were notified in Nov 2025 (13/14 Nov, sources differ); rules 1, 2, 17-21 immediate, rule 4 (consent managers) after 1 year, rules 3, 5-16, 22, 23 (notice, consent, rights) after 18 months, i.e. about 13 May 2027 — [BDO](https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/news/2025/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-%E2%80%94-check-provisions,-implementation-plan,-penalties); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/centre-notifies-dpdp-rules-implementation-planned-in-phases-spread-over-12-18-months-3798465); [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.in/globalassets/1.-member-firms/india/assets/pdfs/flyers/dpdp-rules-nov-2025.pdf)
- DPDP penalties: Act ceiling reported up to Rs 250 crore for security-safeguard failures; category mapping in secondary sources is ambiguous — [BDO](https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/news/2025/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-%E2%80%94-check-provisions,-implementation-plan,-penalties)
- DPDP commentary: purpose-specific consent, marketing consent separate from service consent, no denying core service for refusing marketing, withdrawal as easy as giving, notice before consent; s.17(3) startup relief is only an enabling power, not blanket — [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/md/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-compliance-for-startups-msmes/); [Certinal](https://www.certinal.com/blog/dpdp-privacy-notice-requirements); [Consent.in](https://www.consent.in/blog/dpdp-exemptions)

### Inferences
- Minimum safeguards: unticked marketing checkbox with plain wording ("send me offers on WhatsApp/SMS/email"); store date/time/source of consent; honour STOP/unsubscribe immediately; do not message bought lists; use a DLT-registered SMS provider rather than sending bulk SMS from a personal number; do not add customers to WhatsApp groups/broadcasts without opt-in; published privacy notice.
- A manual one-to-one reply to a customer who messaged first is low-risk; bulk broadcasts to a list are where TRAI/WhatsApp rules apply.
- IT Act: no specific anti-spam section found in sources; (the old s.66A was struck down in 2015 — from background knowledge, not sourced here).

### Gaps
- Meta's official policy pages not fetched; TRAI amendment text and exact promotional header/series rules (e.g. 140/160 series) not confirmed; small-sender penalty under TCCCPR (disconnection, blacklisting) not verified; email spam law in India (no dedicated statute found) not sourced.

## 3. Advertising, influencer and endorsement rules

### Takeaway
Any ad (including Instagram posts) must be truthful; influencers must disclose paid/gifted material connections prominently. Penalties under CCPA go up to Rs 10 lakh (first) and Rs 50 lakh (repeat), with endorser bans possible; ASCI is self-regulatory but names and escalates non-compliers.

### Cited Findings
- Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements, issued 9 June 2022 under s.18 Consumer Protection Act 2019; apply to all media and to advertisers, agencies and endorsers; define bait, surrogate and free-claim ads; ads must not claim consumer legal rights as a unique feature — [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/social-welfare/social-awareness/consumer-education/guidelines-for-prevention-of-misleading-advertisements-and-endorsements-for-misleading-advertisements-2022); [ELP Law](https://elplaw.in/?p=3987)
- Penalty: up to Rs 10 lakh, repeat up to Rs 50 lakh; endorser can be barred up to 1 year (3 years on repeat) — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/influencers-take-note-centre-issues-new-social-media-regulations-with-fines-up-to-rs-50-lakh-366984-2023-01-21); [Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/govt-issues-guidelines-for-misleading-ads-to-impose-up-to-rs-50-lakh-fine-for-violation-120756.html)
- "Endorsement Know-Hows" (Jan 2023, DoCA): influencers must disclose material interest (gifts, free stays, discounts, equity), prominently, not buried in hashtags — [Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/india/2023/01/21/social-media-influencer-mandatory-disclosure-material-interest.html); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/social-media/1278220/new-endorsement-guidelines-for-social-media-influencers)
- ASCI influencer guidelines (May 2021) require labels like #ad / #collaboration / paid-partnership tag; Feb 2025 scorecard: 69% of top 100 influencers non-compliant, 5 escalated to MIB; 14% of flagged ads in FY25 involved influencers — [ASCI scorecard PDF](https://ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Top-Influencer-Compliance-Scorecard-2025-1.pdf); [Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/69-of-indias-top-100-influencers-dont-comply-with-guidelines-asci-study-140688.html); [Exchange4media FY25](https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/14-of-flagged-ads-in-fy25-linked-to-influencers-violations-asci-143887.html)

### Inferences
- Minimum safeguards: no fake "sale"/strikethrough prices, no "only 2 left" unless true, no "100% genuine leather/pure silk" unless provable, no claims of free items with hidden catch; written brief to influencers requiring disclosure; keep proof for fabric/material claims.
- Note a conflict on penalty: one trade headline said "up to Rs 50 lakh"; consistent with first Rs 10 lakh / repeat Rs 50 lakh tiers.

### Gaps
- Consumer Protection Act s.89 criminal penalty for false ads (imprisonment) not retrieved from a source in this search; verify.

## 4. Customer reviews and UGC

### Takeaway
IS 19000:2022 is a voluntary BIS standard (Nov 2022) on collecting and publishing reviews; government has signalled it may become mandatory. Fake/paid reviews can fall under unfair trade practice/misleading ad rules.

### Cited Findings
- IS 19000:2022 applies to any organisation publishing consumer reviews (including sellers collecting from own customers); expects fraud screening, equal moderation of positive and negative, paid/incentivised reviews labelled, right of response, publication within about 72 hours; voluntary initially — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/bis-standards-for-verifying-consumer-reviews-ensuring-transparency-integrity/); [Outlook Business](https://business.outlookindia.com/news/bis-publishes-standard-for-online-consumer-reviews-news-244446); [Vikaspedia](https://vikaspedia.in/social-welfare/social-awareness/consumer-education/centre-launches-framework-for-safeguarding-and-protecting-consumer-interest-from-fake-and-deceptive-reviews-in-e-commerce)
- Reports said fines of Rs 10-50 lakh could apply for fake reviews if framework becomes mandatory (announcement-stage reporting) — [Madhyamam](https://english.madhyamam.com/india/centre-to-crack-down-fake-reviews-online-new-guidelines-expected-1098592); [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/google-review-policy-changes-2026-and-india-s-legal-framework-against-fake-online-reviews)

### Inferences
- Minimum safeguards: never write or buy reviews; do not delete only negative reviews; label reviews given in exchange for discounts; only show real testimonials with permission.

### Gaps
- Whether the review framework was made mandatory by Oct 2026: not found.

## 5. Accessibility

### Takeaway
No specific statute prescribes WCAG for small private shops, but the Supreme Court (30 Apr 2025, Pragya Prasun v Union of India, with Amar Jain) held digital access is part of Article 21 and reportedly applied RPwD Act duties to private entities and regulated entities. Risk for a small shop is low but direction of law is toward accessibility.

### Cited Findings
- Court held right to digital access is part of Art 21; directed regulated entities to follow BIS ICT accessibility standards; reported to bind private entities to provide reasonable accommodation (secondary commentary; judgment not read) — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/bridging-the-digital-divide-indias-evolving-accessibility-framework/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/supreme-court/e-governance-platforms-digital-payment-systems-govt-websites-must-be-accessible-to-persons-with-disabilities-supreme-court-290853); [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/digital-access-as-a-part-of-the-fundamental-right-to-life-and-liberty/print_manually)

### Inferences
- Cheap safeguards: alt text on product photos, sufficient colour contrast, keyboard-usable checkout, resizable text, labelled form fields. GIGW applies to government sites (background knowledge; not sourced).

### Gaps
- Exact scope of the 2025 order for non-regulated small businesses; RPwD penalty provisions; GIGW applicability not sourced.

## 6. Contests, giveaways, discounts, gift cards

### Takeaway
Skill-based/purchase-linked giveaways are safer than chance-based ones that require payment. Prize Competitions Act 1955 is narrow; the 2025 online gaming Act bans online money games. Closed-system gift cards redeemable only on your own store are not RBI-regulated.

### Cited Findings
- Prize Competitions Act 1955: s.4 prohibits prize competitions where monthly prizes exceed Rs 1,000 or entries exceed 2,000; s.5 licence needed for smaller ones; SC (Chamarbaugwala, 1957) read it as covering only competitions of a gambling nature — [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1515/1/A1955-42Eng.pdf); [Venture Intelligence](https://blog.ventureintelligence.com/?p=316)
- Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025 (assent 22 Aug 2025) bans online money games (stakes for monetary return) regardless of skill/chance — [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-bill-2025); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_and_Regulation_of_Online_Gaming_Act,_2025)
- RBI PPI: closed-system instruments (usable only at the issuer, no cash-out, no third-party redemption) are outside RBI authorisation; those redeemable at third parties need authorisation — [RBI FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=126); [Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/default.aspx?id=4972)

### Inferences
- Safeguards: "follow + comment" giveaways with no payment are low-risk but state rules, winner selection, platform disclaimer (Instagram/Meta not sponsor); do not charge entry fees or run "lucky draws" with purchase; own-store gift vouchers fine with an expiry/terms displayed; state-level lottery laws and GST on vouchers not checked.

### Gaps
- Commencement/rules status of the Online Gaming Act and whether Prize Competitions Act is enforced for promotional giveaways; loyalty programme law; gift card expiry norms; GST on vouchers — not found.

## 7. Marketplaces, FDI, regional language

### Takeaway
FDI inventory-model ban matters only for foreign-owned entities; ignore for Indian-owned. E-commerce Rules 2020 require disclosure of seller name/address/grievance contact/country of origin. Legal Metrology declarations apply to online listings.

### Cited Findings
- E-Commerce Rules 2020 (notified 23 July 2020): sellers must disclose legal name, address, customer care and grievance officer, country of origin; marketplaces carry additional duties — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/995148/the-consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020); [Khaitan](https://khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2020-08/Stricter%20Regulations%20on%20E-Commerce%20%E2%80%93%20The%20Consumer%20Protection%20(E-Commerce)%20Rules,%202020.pdf)
- Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) rules since 1 Jan 2018 require declarations on e-commerce listings (manufacturer/packer/importer, commodity name, net quantity, MRP, consumer care details); no dual MRP; example: Rs 1 lakh penalty on an Amazon seller — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/e-commerce-websites-now-come-under-the-purview-of-the-legal-metrology-act/); [Greater Kashmir](https://m.greaterkashmir.com/article/lmd-penalises-amazon-seller-with-rs-1-lakh-penalty/425674)

### Gaps
- FDI rules sources not retrieved; regional-language display requirement: no source found for any website language mandate (Legal Metrology labels require English/Hindi on packages in general - not verified); garment-specific labelling not found.

## 8. Enforcement examples and penalty ranges

### Takeaway
CCPA enforcement so far is mostly small fines on larger platforms for dark patterns, Rs 1-7 lakh each; small shops are unlikely targets but share the same legal duties.

### Cited Findings
- Aug 2026: CCPA fined nine platforms (including Zepto Rs 7 lakh, PhysicsWallah Rs 5 lakh, McAfee Rs 1 lakh, SpiceJet Rs 1 lakh; IndiGo, FirstCry also named) for dark patterns such as drip pricing, basket sneaking, pre-ticked boxes; about Rs 20 lakh total — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/ccpa-fines-9-digital-platforms-including-zepto-indigo-over-dark-patterns); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/ccpa-fines-indigo-zepto-bookmyshow-dark-patterns-quick-commerce-penalty-126080600580_1.html); [Orissa Post](https://www.orissapost.com/ccpa-penalises-indigo-firstcry-physics-wallah-among-nine-platforms-for-dark-patterns/amp/)
- Dark Pattern Guidelines 2023 list 13 patterns (false urgency, confirm shaming, subscription traps, etc.); June 2025 advisory asked e-commerce to self-audit — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/ccpa-fines-9-digital-platforms-including-zepto-indigo-over-dark-patterns)
- CCPA had issued 325 notices and Rs 1.19 crore penalties in its earlier years (undated article) — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/advertising/ccpa-issues-325-notices-for-violation-of-consumer-rights-misleading-ads-imposes-penalties-of-rs-1-19-crore-50910.htm)

### Inferences
- Realistic small-shop risks, in order: infringement notices/takedowns from brands, marketplace/Meta account suspension for policy breaches, consumer complaints for misleading claims, then regulator fines. Avoid pre-ticked add-ons, fake countdown timers, hidden fees.

### Gaps
- Specific CCPA action against a small fashion seller or for fake reviews: not found.
